Research experiences

Ophthalmology

  • Feb 2026 – present
    Acute Angle-Closure Glaucoma (ACG) & Non-Ophthalmic Anticholinergics

    Supervisors: Dr. Vincent Tony Wong, Dr. Rob Pintwala

    Leading a sex-stratified disproportionality analysis of ACG reporting in patients on non-ophthalmic anticholinergics; independently learned and applied R to conduct sensitivity analyses

  • Feb 2026 – present
    Intraocular Hemorrhage & Antiplatelet Use

    Supervisors: Dr. Vincent Tony Wong, Dr. Rob Pintwala

    Integrating disproportional signals with existing evidence to draft a manuscript raising awareness of intraocular bleeding risk especially with clopidogrel and aspirin, independent of anticoagulant use

  • Sept 2025 – Aug 2026
    Taxane Chemotherapy-Associated Ocular Adverse Events

    Supervisors: Dr. Rob Pintwala, Dr. Ahmed Al-Ghoul

    Calculated odds ratios, chi-square, and Bayesian information criterion for 258 ophthalmic outcomes

    Critically evaluated gaps in evidence, sources of bias, and study limitations for a structured literature review to support rigorous interpretation of significant FAERS signals

  • Apr 2026 – Jul 2026
    Ophthalmic Surgical Wait Times

    Supervisor: Dr. Eduardo Navajas

    Investigated regional trends in ophthalmic surgical wait times across BC, with an emphasis on disparities in access for Indigenous communities

    Advocated for resource allocation to support more ophthalmic care in Indigenous communities

  • Jun 2026 – present
    Intraocular Malignancy

    Collaborating with two medical students to screen 5,561 studies for a systematic review, resolving discrepancies by delegating tasks and communicating our decision-making process clearly

  • Aug 2026 – present
    Visual Outcomes after Craniopharyngioma Surgery

    Screening 1,818 studies to determine predictors of visual improvement and deterioration after craniopharyngioma surgery; synthesizing findings for a systematic review

Oncology care

  • Sept 2025 – Jul 2026
    Social Isolation & Cancer Survival

    Supervisor: Dr. John-Jose Nunez

    Recent cancer diagnosis in my mother motivated me to research the effects of psychosocial factors such as the many dimensions of social isolation on cancer survival

    Led an analysis of a population cohort of 45,651 patients using Python; found that living alone, no regular social contact, and depressive or anxiety symptoms significantly increase mortality in cancer

  • Oct 2025 – Sept 2026
    Thymoma Survival & Recurrence

    Supervisor: Dr. Siavash Atrchian

    Using competing risks analysis, estimated the long-term survival and recurrence of thymoma in a population-based cohort in BC followed for up to 21 years

  • Sept 2025 – Jun 2026
    Global Radiotherapy Utilization

    Supervisor: Dr. Jessica Chan

    Screened 3,948 studies for a systematic review and meta-analysis comparing evidence-based radiotherapy need with real-world utilization

    Extracting detailed information to characterize global inequities in access to cancer care

  • Nov 2024 – Sept 2026
    Methadone for Breakthrough Neuropathic Cancer Pain

    Supervisor: Dr. Mathieos Belayneh

    Led a standardized chart review of 706 patients; identified methadone as the second-most common analgesic in BC for breakthrough neuropathic cancer pain

    Data supported continued use of methadone as there were no documented severe adverse events

  • Apr 2024 – Jan 2026
    Rectal Squamous Cell Carcinoma (rSCC) in BC

    Supervisor: Dr. Jeffrey Sulpher

    Collaborated with another medical student to analyze rSCC treatment patterns and outcomes in BC by standardizing data extraction and meeting regularly to resolve chart-review conflicts

  • Dec 2025 – Jul 2026
    Chemoprevention Strategies in Transplant Recipients

    Supervisor: Dr. Fatemeh Jafarian

    Investigated strategies associated with reduced cutaneous malignancy in solid organ transplant recipients

    Coordinated literature review and data extraction across a team of 5 medical students, delegating individual tasks to ensure smooth synthesis of evidence

Koh Tao, Thailand is my favourite place to dive, and where most of the photographs and videos below were taken.

Diving taught me to be observant of the small things. I learned to check my equipment systematically, recognize subtle changes in my surroundings, and anticipate potential problems before they became dangerous.
